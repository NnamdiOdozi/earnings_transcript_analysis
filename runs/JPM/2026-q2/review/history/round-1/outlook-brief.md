# Outlook Brief: JPM — 2026-q2 (30 June 2026)

## 1. Outlook in brief

This brief covers JPMorganChase's results for the 3 months to 30 Jun 2026. Excluding the quarter's significant items, the firm earned \$16.9 billion, or \$6.14 per share, at a 23% return on tangible common equity, on revenue up 15% year-on-year [claim-001][claim-002]. These are adjusted figures: the transcript itself corrects "Including" to "[Excluding]", and a post-event estimates page shows a reported quarterly EPS of 7.70, which appears to include the significant items [claim-001][claim-134].

Our reading is that the next reported period should remain strong but is unlikely to repeat this quarter's peak. The full-year guidance was raised on two fronts at once: net interest income (NII) now runs to about \$105.5 billion in total and about \$96.5 billion excluding Markets, and the adjusted expense outlook to about \$107.5 billion [claim-023][claim-024]. The NII raise rests mainly on balances rather than rates [claim-045]. The revenue spike, by contrast, rests largely on an Equities result management itself called hard to repeat [claim-018][claim-031]. The most likely path for the rest of 2026 is therefore a solid NII-driven run rate with Markets and investment banking revenue normalising from a very high base.

## 2. What changed

Three things moved since the last guidance. First, NII guidance went up. Excluding Markets it was raised from \$95 billion to \$96.5 billion, and total NII rose from the \$103 billion a pre-event preview attributed to management to approximately \$105.5 billion [claim-043][claim-131][claim-023][claim-160]. Barnum said most of the increase came from balances, with higher rates contributing a little [claim-045].

Second, expense guidance was raised by \$2.5 billion. Of that, \$1.5 billion is already booked against capital-markets revenue that beat the firm's own expectations by \$6.5 billion in the first half, and about \$1 billion is implicitly added for the second half [claim-072][claim-075][claim-076].

Third, the quarter beat expectations. Adjusted EPS of \$6.14 came in above the whole pre-event consensus range of \$5.50 to \$5.59 [claim-001][claim-129][claim-159]. That comparison needs care, because the consensus basis is not stated and the preview carrying it is timestamped after JPM's release, although its content is a genuine preview [claim-129][claim-159]. Not everything moved favourably: the CET1 ratio fell 20 basis points to 14.1% as higher RWA and capital distributions outweighed earnings [claim-005]. Revenue can only be compared approximately: consensus of \$49.16 billion to \$51.09 billion implied 12% to 13% growth, against adjusted growth of 15%, but the call gave no dollar total and the two growth rates may not share a basis [claim-130][claim-002].

## 3. Management guidance

Management gave explicit full-year 2026 guidance on four measures, all stated as approximations rather than point estimates:

- NII excluding Markets of about \$96.5 billion, total NII of approximately \$105.5 billion, and Markets NII of about \$9 billion [claim-023].

- An adjusted expense outlook of about \$107.5 billion, raised mainly for volume- and revenue-related expense [claim-024].

- A Card net charge-off rate of approximately 3.2%, reflecting better-than-expected consumer credit [claim-025].

- Consumer deposit growth in the low-to-mid single digits, reaffirmed as unchanged [claim-036].

Barnum added that the NII guide implies a higher exit run rate in the central case, but only if forward rates and deposit drivers play out as expected [claim-044]. The Board intends to raise the quarterly dividend to \$1.65 per share from the third quarter [claim-007]. On investment banking, management expects activity levels to stay healthy but tied conversion to market conditions [claim-016], and Barnum acknowledged some pull-forward into this quarter [claim-030].

## 4. Business drivers

**Markets carried the quarter.** Equities revenue rose 86% and Fixed Income 6% [claim-018][claim-017]. Barnum attributed the Equities result to major IPOs, index rebalancing, Korean equity market dynamics and heavy Asia activity [claim-090], and called that combination statistically improbable to repeat [claim-091]. Against peers this was the strongest Equities growth among the three that disclosed it: Bank of America 70%, Goldman Sachs 72% and Citi 45% [claim-161][claim-139][claim-157][claim-147].

**Investment banking was strong but not exceptional relative to peers.** JPM's fees rose 30%, with equity underwriting leading [claim-014]. That trailed Bank of America at 50% and Goldman Sachs at 55% [claim-162][claim-138][claim-156]. Management conceded some pull-forward and a meaningful contribution from large deals, while describing the pipeline as robust [claim-030][claim-015]. Barnum also noted fees had not been at peak levels by historical standards, so they had room to rise [claim-029].

**Deposits drive NII.** Banking & Wealth Management average deposits rose 3% year-on-year on more than 500,000 net new checking accounts [claim-010], and Wholesale deposit growth outperformed expectations in the first half, partly reflecting the lending environment for non-bank financials and data centres [claim-035]. Barnum said actual rate sensitivity is probably higher than the reported earnings-at-risk figure because consumer deposit betas have outperformed the model [claim-046].

**Expenses tracked revenue.** Expenses rose 15%, matching adjusted revenue growth [claim-003][claim-002][claim-163]. Management's argument is that the variable expense base is tied to capital-markets activity [claim-074], and Dimon called perpetual operating leverage "a crazy notion" [claim-055]. The peer comparison makes the point sharper: Bank of America reported 6.6% operating leverage, and Citi and Wells Fargo both reported positive operating leverage [claim-137][claim-148][claim-150][claim-163]. Our reading is that JPM is reinvesting more of its revenue growth than its peers, which are, for now, converting more of theirs into earnings.

**Capital is ample but being consumed.** Standardized RWA rose approximately \$103 billion, driven by Markets financing and lending [claim-006], and the CIB received a larger equity allocation [claim-049]. Dimon said roughly \$40 billion of excess capital could be deployed over time, with a goal of a 17% return on it [claim-100][claim-103]. JPM's 14.1% CET1 ratio remains above Bank of America's 11.2%, Citi's 12.8% and Wells Fargo's 10.3%, though the ratios may be on different bases [claim-164][claim-005][claim-140][claim-145][claim-152].

**Consumer credit is benign.** Spend is robust across income segments, delinquencies are a little lower than expected, and the firm's data give little support to a K-shaped consumer story [claim-096][claim-097].

## 5. Base case

If deposit balances hold and forward rates play out roughly as priced [claim-044][claim-045] → NII tracks the raised guide of approximately \$105.5 billion [claim-023] → earnings stay strong even as Equities revenue falls back from an 86% growth quarter that management does not expect to repeat [claim-018][claim-091] → observable as quarterly NII consistent with the guide and a Markets line that is lower but still healthy.

In this case expenses land near the guided figure of about \$107.5 billion [claim-024], and Card losses near the approximately 3.2% guide [claim-025]. Returns stay high but below this quarter's adjusted 23% ROTCE [claim-001]. The base case depends most on deposit behaviour, where management says repricing risk is in its models but the question is when [claim-067].

## 6. Upside case

If the active market environment persists [claim-081] and the investment banking pipeline converts as management hopes [claim-016][claim-030] → capital-markets revenue keeps exceeding plan, as it did by \$6.5 billion in the first half [claim-075] → the higher marginal margin on incremental revenue, which an analyst put at 77%, lifts earnings faster than expenses [claim-080][claim-070] → observable as further upward revisions to Markets revenue and positive operating leverage in later quarters.

A second upside route runs through capital. If the firm deploys its roughly \$40 billion of excess capital at close to its 17% return goal [claim-100][claim-103], growth in loans and client financing would add to NII. Regulatory relief is a third possible lever: Dimon set out specific changes he wants to capital rules [claim-059], but nothing in this run's evidence shows they will happen.

## 7. Downside case

If rates rise far enough to force a massive repricing of the deposit franchise, a risk management says is in its models though the timing is unknown [claim-065][claim-067] → the deposit franchise has to pay up to defend balances → NII falls short of the raised guide [claim-023] → observable as deposit outflows to yield-seeking products, which Barnum already calls a modest risk [claim-037].

A second downside is a turn in the cycle. Dimon said conditions are close to as good as it gets and that he does not know how long they will last [claim-063]. He also described a mild deterioration in credit underwriting across the market, including more PIK and weaker covenants, and expects outliers rather than a bell curve of performance when credit turns [claim-115][claim-116]. Barnum flagged consumers with negative real wage growth as a watch area [claim-098]. In that scenario, capital-markets revenue would fall faster than the variable cost base adjusts [claim-074], leaving the raised expense guide [claim-072] exposed.

The strongest evidence against our base case is the scale of the Equities and investment banking contribution itself. Management conceded pull-forward and exceptional events [claim-030][claim-031], so part of this quarter's 15% adjusted revenue growth may simply not recur [claim-002]. We still lead with an NII-driven base case because the NII raise rests on balances, not on Markets [claim-045].

## 8. What to monitor

- Quarterly NII against the approximately \$105.5 billion full-year guide, and whether the implied higher exit run rate materialises [claim-023][claim-044].

- Consumer deposit growth against the low-to-mid single-digit guide, and any sign of accelerating deposit rates paid [claim-036][claim-065].

- Equities revenue as this quarter's exceptional drivers fade, and whether investment banking activity stays healthy as management expects [claim-091][claim-016].

- Expenses against the guide of about \$107.5 billion, and how much of any revenue upside converts to earnings [claim-024][claim-076].

- The CET1 ratio after the 20 basis point decline, as RWA growth continues [claim-005][claim-006].

- Card net charge-offs against the approximately 3.2% guide, and the cohort of consumers with negative real wage growth that management flags as a watch area [claim-025][claim-098].

- The capital rule changes Dimon called for, and any Smart Cash launch this year [claim-059][claim-109].

## 9. Uncertainties and missing evidence

- **Adjusted versus reported basis.** The headline figures exclude significant items that the call did not quantify. A post-event page suggests reported EPS of 7.70 against the adjusted \$6.14 [claim-001][claim-134].

- **Revenue surprise only approximate.** Adjusted growth of 15% exceeds the 12% to 13% growth consensus implied, but the call gave no dollar revenue total, so the comparison may mix bases [claim-130][claim-002].

- **Timing of consensus evidence.** The only usable pre-event consensus source is timestamped after JPM's release, although its content is a preview [claim-129]. A second aggregator page already showed the actual results and was excluded as an expectation source.

- **Third-party regulatory figures.** The preview's G-SIB requirement of 5.2% by 2028 is its own characterisation, not a figure JPM gave in this run's evidence [claim-133].

- **Peer bases differ.** JPM's comparisons use adjusted figures, and peers' CET1 ratios may be on different bases; Goldman Sachs reports ROE rather than ROTCE [claim-165][claim-164].

- **Undisclosed expense detail.** Barnum declined to disclose the marketing-strategy component of expense movements [claim-121].
