# Outlook Brief: LLOY — 2026-h1 (30 June 2026)

## 1. Outlook in brief

This brief covers Lloyds Banking Group's results for the 6 months to 30 Jun 2026, presented alongside a new strategic plan, Accelerate 2030. Statutory profit after tax was £3.1 billion at a 17.1% return on tangible equity, and net income rose 9% to £9.7 billion [claim-011][claim-012].

Our reading is that 2026 should land within the guidance management reiterated rather than raised: net interest income greater than £14.9 billion, a cost:income ratio below 50% and RoTE above 16% [claim-028][claim-035][claim-039][claim-175]. The larger change is the medium-term frame. Management targets a RoTE of around 20% in 2030, and more than 18% in 2028, built on a structural hedge rate it says sits about 50 basis points below market-implied rates [claim-080][claim-066]. That conservatism is the main reason we see more upside than downside to the plan's income line, while costs are the line to watch.

## 2. What changed

Distributions stepped up. The interim dividend rose 30% to 1.58 pence per share, and Lloyds launched its first interim share buyback, of £1 billion, together over £1.9 billion for the half [claim-042][claim-016]. Chalmers said buybacks remain the preferred route for excess capital because management sees value in the stock [claim-082].

The 2026 guidance did not move. The NII guide of greater than £14.9 billion was already set at Q1, when it was lifted from around £14.9 billion, and was reiterated alongside RoTE and capital generation guidance [claim-148][claim-028][claim-175].

The strategy is new. Accelerate 2030 targets mid-single-digit net income CAGR, a high single-digit OOI CAGR, a cost:income ratio below 45% in 2030, and more than 225 basis points of capital generation in 2030 [claim-046][claim-045][claim-047]. Lloyds is also moving Halifax customers under the Lloyds brand [claim-049].

The first reaction from analysts was that the 2028 target looked light. An analyst said the greater-than-18% 2028 RoTE was a bit light versus consensus, which management attributed to prudence and a larger equity base [claim-090][claim-174][claim-092].

## 3. Management guidance

For 2026, management guides to:

- Net interest income greater than £14.9 billion, and structural hedge income greater than £7 billion in 2026 and greater than £8 billion in 2027 [claim-028][claim-027].

- A cost:income ratio below 50% [claim-035].

- An asset quality ratio of around 25 basis points [claim-037].

- RoTE of more than 16%, capital generation of more than 200 basis points, and a pay-down to a 13% CET1 ratio by year end [claim-039][claim-041].

For the plan period:

- Net income growing at mid-single-digit CAGR, with growth higher than that in 2027, and OOI at high single-digit CAGR [claim-046][claim-075][claim-045].

- RoTE of more than 18% in 2028 rising to around 20% in 2030 [claim-080].

- Capital generation rising from greater than 200 basis points in 2026 to around 225 in 2028 [claim-081].

- An asset quality ratio of 25 to 30 basis points throughout the plan [claim-071].

- Around £2 billion of gross cost savings from 2027 to 2030, with cash investment up around 10-15% in 2027 and above £13 billion over the plan [claim-070][claim-068].

## 4. Business drivers

**The structural hedge carries NII.** First-half NII rose 9% to £7.3 billion and the Q2 margin reached 322 basis points [claim-024][claim-025]. Hedge earnings were £3.4 billion on a £246 billion notional at a 2.7% yield [claim-026]. Management expects hedge income to grow to more than £9 billion in 2030, and the plan assumes a 3.7% reinvestment rate against market-implied rates around 50 basis points higher [claim-074][claim-066].

**Other income is diversifying the mix.** Other income rose 11% to £3.3 billion, with insurance, pensions and investments up 19% [claim-029][claim-031]. One drag is visible: operating lease depreciation rose £63 million in Q2, £41 million of it from used car prices falling more than expected [claim-117].

**Costs are flat now and rise next year.** First-half operating costs were flat at £4.9 billion [claim-013]. Opex will increase in 2027 from 1% growth in 2026, and Chalmers put cost growth around 3% over the plan [claim-069][claim-087].

**Credit is benign.** The first-half impairment charge was £617 million, an asset quality ratio of 25 basis points [claim-014].

**Against peers, returns are mid-pack and costs are higher.** Lloyds' first-half RoTE was above Barclays' and Santander's but below NatWest's [claim-170][claim-011][claim-158][claim-169][claim-153]. Its cost:income ratio was higher than NatWest's, HSBC's and Santander's [claim-172][claim-034][claim-155][claim-164][claim-167]. Its pro forma CET1 ratio was the lowest of the five, consistent with its plan to pay down to 13% [claim-171][claim-015][claim-041]. Our reading is that Lloyds' plan depends more than its peers' on future efficiency gains to close the return gap.

## 5. Base case

If rates and deposits behave roughly as the plan assumes [claim-066][claim-143] → hedge income grows as guided toward more than £9 billion in 2030 [claim-074] → net income grows at mid-single digits with a stronger 2027 [claim-046][claim-075] → observable as hedge income above £7 billion this year and NII above £14.9 billion [claim-027][claim-028].

In this case the 2026 cost:income ratio lands below 50%, as Q2's 49% suggests is achievable [claim-035][claim-034], and RoTE stays above 16% [claim-039].

## 6. Upside case

If market rates stay about 50 basis points above the plan's 3.7% hedge assumption [claim-066][claim-091] → refinancing earns more than planned, a benefit management says is not in its circa 20% 2030 RoTE [claim-128] → returns and capital generation run ahead of guidance → observable as hedge yield rising faster than the guided path from about 2.8% in Q2 [claim-110].

Chalmers' rule of thumb shows the leverage: each 1% of RoTE adds roughly 10-15 basis points of capital generation [claim-103]. Nunn also said the cost target leaves room to out-execute [claim-094].

## 7. Downside case

If competition for deposits and mortgages intensifies [claim-077][claim-125] → more of the hedge benefit is passed to customers → net income growth falls toward the bottom of mid-single digits → observable as falling mortgage completion margins, which currently round to around 70 basis points [claim-019].

A second route is the economy. The plan assumes unemployment peaks around 5.5% in early 2027, after Lloyds cut its GDP assumption at Q1 [claim-067][claim-149]. A sharper rise would test the 25 to 30 basis point asset quality range [claim-071].

The strongest evidence against our base case is cost. The first-half cost:income ratio was 50.4%, above the below-50% full-year target, and costs step up in 2027 [claim-034][claim-069]. Peers already run lower ratios [claim-172]. We still lead with the base case because Q2 ran at 49% and management reiterated the 2026 target [claim-034][claim-035].

## 8. What to monitor

- Quarterly NIM, which management expects to rise in every quarter of 2026 [claim-142].

- Hedge income against the greater-than £7 billion 2026 guide [claim-027].

- The cost:income ratio against the below-50% 2026 target [claim-035].

- Operating lease depreciation and used car prices [claim-117].

- Deposit growth, which Nunn expects to be slightly slower than over the last five years [claim-125].

- Capital generation against the greater-than 200 basis point guide [claim-041].

## 9. Uncertainties and missing evidence

- **No first-half consensus for profit or RoTE.** The only pre-results consensus in this run is full-year revenue, and its definition need not match Lloyds' own [claim-152].

- **Peer timing and bases.** NatWest reported the day after Lloyds' event, and peer return and cost measures are defined differently [claim-153][claim-172].

- **AI benefits are not quantified to 2030.** Management linked earlier revenue and cost targets to digital and AI only on a broad definition [claim-130].
