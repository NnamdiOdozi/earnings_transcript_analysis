# Outlook Brief: LLOY — 2026-h1 (30 June 2026)

## 1. Outlook in brief

The headline figures cover the 6 months to 30 Jun 2026. Lloyds enters the second half with net income of £9.7 billion, return on tangible equity of 17.1%, flat operating costs, and 108 basis points of capital generation [claim-001][claim-002][claim-003][claim-005].

The near-term direction remains positive but should be less even than the first-half headline suggests. Management still expects 2026 net interest income above £14.9 billion, return on tangible equity above 16%, an asset quality ratio around 25 basis points, a cost-income ratio below 50%, and capital generation above 200 basis points [claim-011][claim-012][claim-013][claim-014][claim-028]. Other income should continue to grow in the third quarter, but more slowly than in the second quarter because the mortgage-securitisation benefit will not repeat [claim-026].

Beyond 2026, the case rests on continued net-interest-income growth, high-single-digit other-income growth, and cost savings funding a larger investment programme. These are management targets, not independently forecast outcomes [claim-015][claim-016][claim-017].

## 2. What changed

First-half net income increased 9% year-on-year to £9.7 billion. Net interest income increased 9% to £7.3 billion, while other income increased 11% to £3.3 billion [claim-002][claim-008][claim-010]. Net interest margin reached 319 basis points for the first half and 322 basis points for the second quarter [claim-009].

Capital returns stepped up. Lloyds increased the interim dividend by 30% to 1.58 pence per share and announced its first £1 billion interim buyback [claim-006][claim-007].

The strategic horizon also changed. Accelerate 2030 introduces targets for mid-single-digit net income growth, high-single-digit other-income growth, a cost-income ratio below 45%, return on tangible equity around 20%, and capital generation above 225 basis points by 2030 [claim-015]. It pairs those targets with more than £13 billion of cash investment and around £2 billion of gross cost savings over the plan [claim-016][claim-017].

The source pack does not support a clean half-year beat-or-miss conclusion. A pre-event analyst page gives a 2026 revenue forecast of £21.80 billion, but that full-year measure is not directly comparable with Lloyds' first-half net income [claim-025].

## 3. Management guidance

For the 12 months to 31 Dec 2026, management guides to:

- Net interest income above £14.9 billion [claim-011].
- An asset quality ratio around 25 basis points [claim-012].
- Return on tangible equity above 16% [claim-013].
- A cost-income ratio below 50% [claim-028].
- A year-end Common Equity Tier 1 ratio of 13% and capital generation above 200 basis points [claim-014].

For the 3 months to 30 Sep 2026, management expects other-income growth to continue, but at a slower rate than in the second quarter [claim-026].

For the 48 months to 31 Dec 2030, management expects net interest margin to continue rising before broadly stabilising and possibly easing near the end of the plan [claim-020]. The longer-term financial framework targets the outcomes described in section 2 and assumes average real United Kingdom gross domestic product growth of 1.4%, inflation around 2%, nominal gross domestic product growth around 3.5%, and a terminal base rate of 3.5% [claim-015][claim-018].

## 4. Business drivers

Net interest income is supported by the starting margin position and management's expected margin path [claim-008][claim-009][claim-020]. The important counterweight is competition. Management explicitly says it cannot control either the future yield-curve shape or how competitors behave [claim-021].

Other-income diversification is the second driver. First-half other income grew 11%, and management is targeting high-single-digit growth through 2030 [claim-010][claim-015]. Transport should contribute, although operating lease depreciation will also increase as the fleet grows [claim-022].

Efficiency is the third driver. Operating costs were flat in the first half. Management plans around £2 billion of gross cost savings from 2027 to 2030 while increasing cash investment by 10% to 15% in 2027 [claim-003][claim-016][claim-017]. This creates an execution tension. Investment must generate enough income and productivity benefit to improve the cost-income ratio every year.

The balance sheet provides capacity, but not immunity. The pro forma Common Equity Tier 1 ratio was 13.1% after distributions. Management also cites a 46% average mortgage loan-to-value ratio and an approximately 90% secured small-business lending portfolio [claim-005][claim-019].

## 5. Base case

The base case is continued income growth through the rest of 2026, with net interest income doing more of the near-term work and other income growing at a slower sequential rate in the third quarter [claim-011][claim-026]. Credit remains consistent with the approximately 25-basis-point full-year asset-quality-ratio guide. Operating leverage remains consistent with the cost-income-ratio guide below 50%. Capital generation supports both the 13% year-end Common Equity Tier 1 target and the announced distributions [claim-012][claim-028][claim-014][claim-006][claim-007].

Over the plan period, the base case is gradual rather than linear improvement. Margin should rise and then plateau. Cost savings should increasingly fund investment. Other-income growth should broaden the revenue mix [claim-020][claim-016][claim-017][claim-015].

## 6. Upside case

The upside case requires stronger income conversion than management's prudent assumptions imply. A more favourable yield curve, less intense competition, or stronger customer-balance growth could sustain net interest margin for longer [claim-020][claim-021]. Faster delivery of the £2 billion gross-cost programme could improve operating leverage while preserving the investment plan [claim-016][claim-017].

Faster customer adoption and timely regulatory approval could also increase the contribution from artificial-intelligence-enabled products and productivity tools [claim-027]. Strong peer performance before Lloyds reported shows that the banking backdrop was capable of supporting growth. Barclays reported second-quarter group income up 16%, while Standard Chartered reported first-half pre-tax profit up 9% and above its analyst average [claim-023][claim-024]. These peers have different reporting periods and business mixes, so they are context rather than direct forecasts for Lloyds.

Observable upside evidence would include margin remaining above the second-quarter level, other-income growth reaccelerating after the third-quarter comparison effect, cost-income improvement despite the investment ramp, and capital generation running above the 2026 floor [claim-009][claim-026][claim-014][claim-016].

## 7. Downside case

The downside case begins with the two factors management says it cannot control: the yield curve and competitive behaviour [claim-021]. Margin pressure could offset more of the structural-hedge benefit and make the long-term margin plateau arrive earlier [claim-020].

Other income also carries mix and execution risk. The second-quarter securitisation benefit will not repeat in the third quarter. Transport growth brings higher operating lease depreciation, even though management expects transport other income to grow faster [claim-026][claim-022].

The 2027 investment step-up creates another risk. Cash investment is expected to rise 10% to 15% before stabilising. If revenue or productivity benefits arrive late, the annual cost-income-ratio improvement could be delayed [claim-016][claim-015]. Artificial-intelligence-led benefits also depend on customer adoption and regulatory approvals [claim-027].

Credit is currently benign, but the long-term plan assumes an asset quality ratio of 25 to 30 basis points. A materially weaker United Kingdom economy would test that range and reduce the capital available for distributions [claim-015][claim-018].

## 8. What to monitor

- Net interest margin against the first-half 319-basis-point and second-quarter 322-basis-point starting points [claim-009].
- Progress toward 2026 net interest income above £14.9 billion [claim-011].
- Third-quarter other-income growth after removal of the mortgage-securitisation benefit [claim-026].
- Operating costs, the 2026 cost-income-ratio guide below 50%, and annual cost-income improvement as investment rises [claim-003][claim-028][claim-015][claim-016].
- Delivery of the £2 billion gross-cost-savings programme [claim-017].
- The asset quality ratio against the 2026 guide and the 25-to-30-basis-point plan range [claim-012][claim-015].
- Common Equity Tier 1 capital, capital generation, dividends, and half-yearly excess-capital decisions [claim-005][claim-006][claim-007][claim-014].
- Transport other income relative to operating lease depreciation [claim-022].
- Customer adoption and regulatory approval of artificial-intelligence-enabled services [claim-027].

## 9. Uncertainties and missing evidence

The pack contains no regulatory financial evidence for `LLOY`; the manifest records United States Securities and Exchange Commission evidence as not applicable. Reported figures therefore rely on the official transcript.

The pack contains no reliable event-specific consensus measure that can be compared directly with the reported half-year figures. The available pre-event analyst evidence is a full-year revenue forecast [claim-025]. No beat-or-miss conclusion is made.

NatWest and HSBC half-year sources in the pack were published after Lloyds' 30 July event. They are not used as pre-event context. Barclays and Standard Chartered reported before Lloyds, but their different geographic and business mixes limit comparability [claim-023][claim-024].

Accelerate 2030 is new guidance with a long time horizon. The evidence establishes management's targets and assumptions, but not the pace at which individual initiatives will contribute. Customer adoption, regulatory approval, the yield curve, and competition remain material uncertainties [claim-021][claim-027].
