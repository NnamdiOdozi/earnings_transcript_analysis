# Outlook Brief: JPM — 2026-q2 (30 June 2026)

## 1. Outlook in brief

This run's headline figures cover the **3 months to 30 Jun 2026**. Excluding
significant items, the firm reported net income of \$16.9 billion, EPS of \$6.14
and an ROTCE of 23% [claim-001], on revenue up 15% year-on-year [claim-002].
That was comfortably ahead of the pre-event consensus of \$5.67 per share and
sat in the upper half of the \$5.09 to \$6.56 estimate range
[claim-055][claim-056][claim-070].

Our interpretation is that this was a genuinely exceptional quarter whose
composition matters more than its size. The evidence suggests the beat was
driven disproportionately by market-sensitive activity that management itself
declines to extrapolate, while the durable, balance-sheet-driven part of the
franchise improved by a much more ordinary amount. The direction over the next
reported period therefore depends less on whether the franchise is performing
well — the evidence says it is — and more on how far capital-markets revenue
normalises from a level management has explicitly described as hard to repeat
[claim-030][claim-031].

For the financial year, management's own raised guidance is the most reliable
anchor available in this evidence, and it points to continued growth in net
interest income alongside a broadly matching increase in expenses
[claim-021][claim-026].

## 2. What changed

Full-year net interest income excluding Markets guidance was revised up from
\$95 billion to \$96.5 billion, with total NII now guided to approximately
\$105.5 billion and Markets NII to about \$9 billion [claim-021][claim-024].

The adjusted expense outlook was raised to about \$107.5 billion [claim-022],
an increase of \$2.5 billion on the prior guide [claim-026].

The Card net charge-off rate outlook was cut to approximately 3.2%, which
management attributed to better-than-expected consumer credit performance
[claim-023].

The Board signalled an increase in the quarterly dividend to \$1.65 per share
effective in the third quarter [claim-007].

Consumer deposit growth guidance was explicitly left unchanged at low-to-mid
single digits [claim-039] — worth noting because it is the one major forward
number management did *not* revise upward in an otherwise uniformly raised
outlook.

## 3. Management guidance

Management gave quantitative full-year guidance across four lines: NII excluding
Markets of about \$96.5 billion, total NII of approximately \$105.5 billion and
Markets NII of about \$9 billion [claim-021]; adjusted expenses of about \$107.5
billion [claim-022]; and a Card net charge-off rate of approximately 3.2%
[claim-023].

Two qualifications attach to that guidance. First, management said the upward
NII revision was driven principally by deposit balances across Wholesale and
Consumer, with rates a smaller contributor [claim-025] — a balance-driven revision
is more durable than a rate-driven one, but it is also more exposed to the
yield-seeking deposit flows management separately flagged as a risk [claim-040].
Second, and much more minor, roughly \$150 million of the Markets NII increase is
not expected to be offset in the bottom line — an exception to the firm's usual
rule, but one management itself called quite small and, at the level of the
company, left pocket to right pocket [claim-054].

On investment banking, guidance was deliberately qualitative: management expects
activity levels to remain healthy while stating that conversion depends on market
conditions [claim-014].

## 4. Business drivers

**Capital markets did the heavy lifting.** CIB revenue rose 27% year-on-year
[claim-011], within which equities revenue rose 86% [claim-016] against Fixed
Income up 6% [claim-015]. Investment banking fees rose 30% [claim-012].

**The consumer franchise grew steadily rather than spectacularly.** CCB revenue
rose 8% year-on-year [claim-008], with Banking & Wealth Management average
deposits up 3% year-on-year and net new checking account growth above 500,000
accounts [claim-009], and client investment assets up 21% [claim-010].

**Asset & Wealth Management compounded.** AWM revenue rose 19% with a 38%
pre-tax margin [claim-017], on \$50 billion of long-term net inflows in the
quarter [claim-018] and AUM of \$5.1 trillion, up 18% [claim-019].

**Costs rose with revenue.** Expenses of \$27.3 billion were up 15% [claim-003],
against revenue growth of 15% excluding significant items [claim-002]. We do not
treat that as an exact like-for-like match: the transcript attaches the
ex-significant-items qualifier to revenue and then states expenses without
restating it, so the two rates may not be struck on the same basis, and the
archived evidence cannot settle it. Management's explanation is that
the capital markets complex outperformed its earlier expectations by \$6.5
billion in the first half, against which \$1.5 billion of additional expense was
booked [claim-027].

**Capital was deployed rather than accumulated — and management says that is the
point.** The standardized CET1 ratio fell 20 basis points to 14.1% as net income
was more than offset by higher RWA and capital distributions [claim-005], with
standardized RWA up approximately \$103 billion on Markets financing and
traditional lending growth [claim-006]. Read alone that looks like a constraint
tightening. Management's own position cuts the other way: the CEO said the firm
still has roughly \$40 billion of excess capital and now thinks it could actually
deploy it over time [claim-043], with the stated goal of deploying capital at a
17% return [claim-044]. On that reading the CET1 decline is intentional
deployment into client growth, not erosion. Our interpretation is that both
readings are live, and the distinction is empirical rather than rhetorical: it
turns on the return the deployed capital earns, which this quarter's evidence
cannot yet show. An analyst pressed the adjacent question directly, asking why
the firm is buying back stock at 3 times tangible book when conditions are this
good [claim-046].

Separately, the firm increased the equity allocation to the CIB during the
quarter [claim-053] — on management's own characterisation of that same passage, a
movement of equity between pockets of the one company [claim-054] rather than a
group capital outflow. It is not evidence either way on the question above.

## 5. Base case

**Conditions.** Capital-markets activity normalises part-way from this quarter's
level rather than either holding or collapsing, the labour market stays resilient
so consumer credit continues to behave [claim-037], and deposit balances hold up
broadly in line with the unchanged consumer growth expectation [claim-039].

**Mechanism.** The revised NII guidance is balance-driven rather than rate-driven
[claim-025], so it survives a partial normalisation of trading and banking
revenue. Expenses, by contrast, carry a large revenue-linked variable component
[claim-027], so a fall in capital-markets revenue takes some of its own cost base
with it.

**Consequence.** Reported earnings step down from this quarter's level without
the underlying franchise deteriorating, and full-year outcomes land near the
guided NII and expense figures [claim-021][claim-022].

**Observable indicators.** NII excluding Markets tracking toward the raised
\$96.5 billion full-year figure [claim-021]; the Card net charge-off rate holding
at or below the guided approximately 3.2% [claim-023]; equities revenue falling
back from its 86% year-on-year growth rate without dragging fee businesses with
it [claim-016].

The strongest evidence *against* this base case is that it takes management's
"normalisation without deterioration" framing largely at face value. The
counter-reading is set out in section 7, and one piece of it — the firm's
investment banking fee growth trailing its closest peers [claim-073] — is
genuinely awkward for the view that this franchise is simply harvesting an
industry-wide boom better than others. We lead with the base case above
nonetheless, because the guidance revisions that are *least* dependent on
market conditions (deposit-driven NII [claim-025], a reduced card loss rate
[claim-023]) all moved favourably, and those are the parts of the outlook the
evidence supports most directly.

## 6. Upside case

**Conditions.** The pipeline conversion management describes as robust actually
materialises [claim-013], and the dynamic management described — current activity
levels encouraging more activity [claim-013] — persists into the second half.

**Mechanism.** Investment banking fees grew more slowly than the trading line
this quarter [claim-012][claim-016], and management says they were not at super
peak levels by historical standards and had some room to come up [claim-076]. A
fee-led rather than trading-led second half would be more repeatable and less
capital-intensive than this quarter's mix.

**Consequence.** Revenue holds nearer this quarter's run-rate with a better
quality of earnings, and the capital deployed this quarter [claim-005] earns
closer to the 17% return management set as its goal [claim-044].

**Observable indicators.** Investment banking fee growth closing the gap to
peers rather than widening it [claim-073]; announced-deal conversion continuing
at the "healthy" activity levels management guided to [claim-014]; and the 20
basis point CET1 decline recorded this quarter [claim-005] being followed by
evidence that the capital went out at the intended return [claim-044] rather than
by a further unexplained decline.

## 7. Downside case

**Conditions.** The equities result proves to be the peak management implies it
is [claim-030][claim-031], *and* the second-half banking pipeline turns out to
have been partially consumed by this quarter's pull-forward [claim-029].

**Mechanism.** Both of the quarter's two largest revenue accelerants reverse at
once, while the expense base does not fall as fast: \$1.5 billion of the \$2.5
billion expense guidance increase is already booked and therefore not reversible
if revenue falls [claim-028][claim-074]. A separate, slower channel operates
through deposits, but only on a materially higher trigger than rates merely
staying elevated: management framed the convexity risk around a true change in
rate regime, illustrated with a 7% rate environment, and said the effect has not
appeared so far in this cycle [claim-038]. Were such a regime to arrive, a
repricing of the deposit franchise could be forced, against yield-seeking flows
already identified as a live risk [claim-040].

**Consequence.** Negative operating leverage of the kind an analyst already put
to management on year-to-date figures [claim-047] reasserts itself, with a
higher expense base carried against a normalised revenue line.

**Observable indicators.** Equities revenue reverting sharply from its 86%
growth rate [claim-016]; investment banking fees falling rather than converting
from the pipeline [claim-012]; consumer deposit growth undershooting the
unchanged low-to-mid single digit expectation [claim-039]; any deterioration in
the cohort with negative real wage growth management named as a watch area
[claim-036]; and, more slowly, the "very mild" credit underwriting deterioration
the CEO described [claim-041] broadening beyond the individual data-centre deals
the firm declined [claim-042].

A second downside channel is not market-driven at all. Management said the
succession timetable is unchanged at "several years, plus or minus" and entirely
the Board's decision [claim-051], following the appointment of two Co-Presidents
[claim-052]. This is a key-person exposure that no quarterly number captures.

## 8. What to monitor

- **NII exit run-rate versus the \$96.5 billion full-year figure** — management
  attributed the revision principally to deposit balances, and that driver mix
  (balances versus rates) determines how durable it is [claim-021][claim-025].
- **Card net charge-off rate against the approximately 3.2% guide** — the one
  credit number management actively improved this period [claim-023].
- **Equities revenue normalisation from 86% year-on-year growth** — the single
  largest swing factor in the quarter and the one management most explicitly
  refused to extrapolate [claim-016][claim-031].
- **Investment banking fee growth relative to peers** — JPMorganChase's 30%
  [claim-012] against Bank of America's 50% [claim-065] is the clearest
  competitive signal in this evidence [claim-073].
- **CET1 rebuild against RWA growth, tested against the returns it buys** — a
  further decline from 14.1% alongside continued RWA expansion [claim-005][claim-006]
  matters only if the deployed capital does not earn the 17% return management set
  as the goal [claim-044]; against the roughly \$40 billion of excess capital
  management says it still holds [claim-043], the same decline reads as intended
  deployment. Watch which of the two the returns support.
- **Second-half expense delivery against the \$107.5 billion outlook** —
  specifically whether the \$1 billion implicitly added for the second half is
  spent as revenue-linked cost or as discretionary investment [claim-022][claim-028].
- **Token and AI-related expense** — trivial for the full year on management's own
  account and explicitly not a meaningful driver of the current outlook or of the
  revision to it [claim-048], but forecast to accelerate meaningfully in the second
  half [claim-048] and named by management as an important question going into next
  year and the subsequent years [claim-077]. A forward cost line to watch, not a
  current one.
- **Deposit repricing behaviour if rates rise further** — the convexity effect
  management said has not yet appeared but must eventually [claim-038].

## 9. Uncertainties and missing evidence

**The headline basis is not the statutory basis.** The \$6.14 EPS management
framed the quarter around excludes significant items; the diluted EPS
subsequently filed with the SEC for the same quarter was \$7.70 [claim-071]. Any
comparison of "the beat" therefore depends on which basis is used, and the
consensus source this run cites does not state which basis its \$5.67 estimate is
struck on [claim-055]. This is a real limitation on the precision of the surprise
claim, not a defect in either source.

**No total revenue figure was stated on the call.** Management gave a growth rate
[claim-002] and segment-level revenues, but no consolidated revenue number, so
the revenue side of the consensus comparison [claim-055] cannot be closed from
this evidence at all. Only the EPS side of the surprise is verifiable here.

**Peer results were same-day, not prior.** The four peer sources used here were
published on the day of the call (each is dated 14 July 2026 in the
archived source). Goldman Sachs' numbers were demonstrably
available to management, who said so on the call [claim-075][claim-060], but the
Bank of America [claim-062], Citigroup [claim-067] and Wells Fargo [claim-069]
results should be read as *our* comparative context, not as information
management had when framing its own outlook.

**Two archived web sources were excluded on temporal grounds.** Both carried
provider metadata that did not mark them post-event, but their content contained
post-event actuals for this very quarter. Using either as a pre-event
"expectation" would have manufactured a circular surprise. The consensus figures
cited here come only from the source published before the event [claim-055].

**Management opinion is doing real work in this brief.** The judgment that the
equities result is unlikely to repeat is management's own [claim-030][claim-031],
and it is convenient for them: it lowers the bar for subsequent quarters. We
have relied on it because the peer evidence independently supports a
market-driven rather than firm-specific explanation [claim-072], but it remains a
self-assessment, not an externally verified one.

**Weakly supported:** the upside case depends on pipeline conversion
[claim-013][claim-014], for which this evidence contains only management's
qualitative characterisation and no order-book or backlog figure of any kind.
