# Outlook Brief: JPM — 2026-q2 (30 June 2026)

## 1. Outlook in brief

For the 3 months to 30 June 2026, JPMorgan combined broad operating strength with an unusually favorable capital-markets environment. Net income excluding significant items was \$16.9 billion, EPS was \$6.14 and return on tangible common equity was 23% [claim-001]. Reported EPS exceeded two pre-event consensus estimates [claim-074][claim-075][claim-076].

The near-term outlook remains constructive, but the quality of that outlook depends on separating repeatable franchise gains from unusually strong markets activity. Deposit balances, client acquisition, investment-banking pipelines, asset inflows and resilient consumer behavior support continued earnings strength [claim-010][claim-014][claim-018][claim-027][claim-054]. Against that, management itself said conditions were close to “as good as it gets,” warned that the exact Equities backdrop was difficult to repeat, and acknowledged that underwriting standards have weakened mildly in parts of the market [claim-041][claim-051][claim-066].

## 2. What changed

Management raised full-year net interest income guidance. Net interest income excluding Markets moved from \$95 billion to \$96.5 billion. Total net interest income is now expected to be approximately \$105.5 billion, including about \$9 billion from Markets [claim-020][claim-032]. Deposit balances and favorable mix were the main reasons for the increase. Higher rates were a smaller contributor [claim-033].

The stronger revenue environment also raised costs. Adjusted expense guidance increased to approximately \$107.5 billion [claim-021]. Management explained that the capital-markets complex had outperformed prior expectations by \$6.5 billion during the first half, accompanied by \$1.5 billion of additional expense. Of the \$2.5 billion guidance increase, \$1.5 billion had already been incurred and \$1 billion was added for the second half [claim-044][claim-045]. The evidence therefore points to positive marginal economics rather than an uncontrolled cost increase, although the higher expense base still raises the hurdle if revenue normalizes [claim-043][claim-107].

Consumer-credit guidance improved. The expected Card net charge-off rate is now approximately 3.2%, reflecting better-than-expected credit performance [claim-022]. The quarterly dividend is also intended to rise to \$1.65 per share in the third quarter [claim-007].

## 3. Management guidance

The explicit full-year guidance is approximately \$105.5 billion of total net interest income, \$96.5 billion excluding Markets, adjusted expenses of about \$107.5 billion, and a Card net charge-off rate near 3.2% [claim-020][claim-021][claim-022]. Management also continues to expect low-to-mid-single-digit consumer deposit growth during 2026 [claim-028].

Investment-banking activity is expected to remain healthy if market conditions permit. That guidance is qualified by both a robust pipeline and some pull-forward of large transactions into the reported quarter [claim-014][claim-024]. Management offered no numerical revenue forecast for Markets and was explicit that the particular Equities combination could prove difficult to repeat [claim-025][claim-051].

Longer-term guidance was more directional. Expense growth might slow in 2027 or 2028, but management did not commit to that outcome because it intends to keep funding attractive investments [claim-031][claim-037]. Artificial-intelligence token expense should accelerate meaningfully in the second half, although it remains trivial in the full-year expense outlook [claim-069].

## 4. Business drivers

The strongest repeatable driver appears to be franchise breadth. Consumer & Community Banking revenue rose 8%, Corporate & Investment Bank revenue rose 27%, and Asset & Wealth Management revenue rose 19% for the reported 3-month period [claim-008][claim-012][claim-017]. Banking & Wealth Management added more than 500,000 net new checking accounts, while average deposits rose both year on year and sequentially [claim-010]. Wholesale deposits also exceeded management's expectations during the first half [claim-027].

Markets strength was real but unusually concentrated. Fixed Income revenue rose 6%, while Equities revenue rose 86% [claim-015][claim-016]. Major initial public offerings, index rebalancing, Korean-market dynamics and high Asian activity contributed to the Equities outcome [claim-051]. This supports a constructive near-term fee environment but argues against simply annualizing the quarter.

Consumer evidence remains supportive. Spending was robust across income groups and delinquencies were below expectations across credit-score cohorts [claim-054]. Management saw little support in its own data for a sharply K-shaped consumer picture [claim-055]. The important counterweight is that negative real wage growth remains a watch area for some consumers, while labor-market resilience remains central to future credit performance [claim-056][claim-057].

Artificial intelligence is likely to affect both capacity and costs. JPMorgan has almost 1,000 use cases, with roughly 50 viewed as especially important, and has already reduced staffing by 30% to 40% in some functions while offering most affected employees other roles [claim-048][claim-049]. Management nevertheless expects competition to pass much of the benefit to customers rather than allowing permanently higher returns [claim-050].

## 5. Base case

The base case is continued franchise-led growth with lower incremental momentum than the reported quarter. If deposit balances remain firm, consumer credit remains tied to a resilient labor market, and the investment-banking pipeline converts at a healthy but non-exceptional rate [claim-014][claim-027][claim-033][claim-057], then higher net interest income and broad fee generation should offset much of the normalization in Equities. The consequence would be strong returns without requiring a repeat of the quarter's most exceptional trading conditions. Observable indicators are consumer and wholesale deposit growth, Card delinquencies, investment-banking conversion and the mix of Markets revenue.

The strongest evidence against this base case is management's own description of exuberant markets and “as good as it gets” conditions [claim-041][claim-046]. That evidence is persuasive enough to prevent treating the reported growth rates as a steady run rate. It is less persuasive as a case for an immediate earnings reversal because deposit growth, client inflows and checking-account acquisition were separate from the exceptional Equities events [claim-010][claim-018][claim-027].

## 6. Upside case

If the robust investment-banking pipeline generates further activity, consumer and wholesale deposits continue outperforming, and JPMorgan can deploy excess capital into client demand at acceptable returns [claim-014][claim-027][claim-058][claim-071], then revenue breadth could remain stronger for longer. The mechanism would be simultaneous growth in net interest income, investment-banking fees, asset-management fees and client balances. The consequence would be continued strong returns even as individual Markets products normalize. Observable indicators are pipeline conversion, loan and deposit balances, net inflows and management's ability to maintain risk standards [claim-018][claim-073].

Additional upside could come from productive technology investment. Management expects large efficiency gains from artificial intelligence and is building discipline around matching models to tasks and requiring measurable capacity, efficiency, revenue or competitive benefits [claim-048][claim-070]. Evidence of lower error rates, greater processing capacity or slower expense growth without weaker client outcomes would strengthen this case.

## 7. Downside case

If market volumes normalize sharply while the higher expense base persists, then the revenue-linked operating leverage that benefited the quarter would reverse [claim-021][claim-044][claim-046]. The mechanism would be lower Equities and investment-banking revenue against costs already added for activity and investment. The consequence would be weaker operating leverage even if the core franchise remained sound. Observable indicators are Equities volumes, underwriting activity and any further expense-guidance change.

A second downside path comes through rates and credit. If rates rise enough to trigger materially faster deposit repricing, the apparent benefit from higher rates could be offset by funding costs [claim-042][claim-087]. If labor conditions weaken at the same time, consumer credit could deteriorate from its currently better-than-expected position [claim-022][claim-057]. Mild weakening in industry underwriting and the existence of deals outside JPMorgan's risk framework add a separate late-cycle risk [claim-065][claim-066]. Observable indicators are deposit betas, yield-seeking outflows, unemployment, Card delinquencies and covenant or refinancing stress.

## 8. What to monitor

- Consumer and wholesale deposit growth, deposit mix and signs of faster repricing [claim-027][claim-028][claim-029][claim-042].
- Investment-banking pipeline conversion and whether activity remains healthy after the quarter's pull-forward [claim-014][claim-024].
- Equities revenue and client volumes after the exceptional mix of IPOs, rebalancing and Asian activity [claim-016][claim-051].
- Card delinquencies, charge-offs and labor-market resilience [claim-022][claim-054][claim-057].
- Expense growth relative to revenue-linked compensation, technology investment and AI token usage [claim-021][claim-045][claim-069][claim-070].
- Capital deployment, share repurchase discipline and maintenance of underwriting standards [claim-058][claim-059][claim-065][claim-073].
- Progress in European digital banking, including the path from customer growth to breakeven [claim-063][claim-064].

## 9. Uncertainties and missing evidence

Management did not provide a numerical Markets revenue outlook, so the degree of normalization cannot be quantified from this source pack. The company also did not quantify the future cost savings or revenue gains from artificial intelligence. The consumer outlook depends heavily on labor-market resilience, but the call offered no company forecast for unemployment.

Peer results extracted on or after the event date were not used to characterize what was knowable before the call. Several pages were marked undated or “pre-event” only because the provider's metadata operates at day-level resolution. Some contained results that could only have been known after release. This prevents a clean contemporaneous peer comparison and is preferable to introducing hindsight.
