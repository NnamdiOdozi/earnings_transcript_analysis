# Outlook Brief: JPM — 2026-q2-rerun (30 June 2026)

## 1. Outlook in brief

For the 3 months to 30 Jun 2026 the firm reported net income of \$16.9 billion, EPS of \$6.14 and a return on tangible common equity of 23%, excluding the significant items management noted [claim-001], on revenue up 15% year-on-year [claim-002]. That beat both pre-event consensus figures captured in this run [claim-192].

Our interpretation is that the next reported period is more likely to step down than to repeat, and that the step-down would be a market normalisation rather than a company-specific problem. The quarter was carried by an exceptional Equities result [claim-018] that management itself doubts is repeatable [claim-034][claim-108], in conditions every peer in this run shared [claim-197]. What should persist into the full year is the raised net interest income guidance [claim-047] and the improved card credit outlook [claim-026], both of which rest on balance-sheet and credit trends rather than on trading conditions.

## 2. What changed

Two guidance lines moved, in opposite directions for earnings quality.

Full-year net interest income excluding Markets was raised from \$95 billion to \$96.5 billion [claim-047][claim-024], with total net interest income now put at approximately \$105.5 billion and Markets net interest income at about \$9 billion [claim-024]. Management attributed the revision principally to deposit balances across Wholesale and Consumer plus a mix shift toward slightly higher margin [claim-049], with rates a little higher at both ends of the curve than at the prior guide [claim-050].

Against that, the full-year adjusted expense outlook rose to about \$107.5 billion [claim-025], an increase of \$2.5 billion that management explicitly declined to call trivial [claim-086]. The composition matters more than the headline: \$1.5 billion of it is already booked against a first-half capital markets outperformance of \$6.5 billion, with a further \$1 billion implicitly added for the second half [claim-088][claim-087]. Our reading is that the majority of this increase is the mechanical consequence of revenue already earned rather than a fresh discretionary spending decision [claim-199] — though management cautioned against reading the second-half addition as a signal about its own revenue expectations [claim-091].

The card net charge-off outlook improved to approximately 3.2% on better-than-expected consumer credit performance [claim-026], consistent with delinquencies running a little below the firm's expectations across the FICO spectrum [claim-116].

## 3. Management guidance

Quantitative guidance this period covers three lines and one capital objective.

Net interest income: approximately \$96.5 billion excluding Markets and approximately \$105.5 billion in total for the 12 months to 31 Dec 2026 [claim-024], the revised figure implying a higher exit run rate provided the yield curve follows the forwards and deposit drivers match current expectations [claim-048]. Expenses: about \$107.5 billion adjusted [claim-025]. Card credit: a net charge-off rate of approximately 3.2% [claim-026]. Consumer deposit growth guidance of low-to-mid single digits was restated as unchanged [claim-039].

On capital, the Board intends to raise the quarterly dividend to \$1.65 per share from the third quarter [claim-007], and management stated an objective of deploying capital at a 17% return [claim-126]. Investment banking guidance was qualitative only — activity levels expected to remain healthy, with conversion dependent on market conditions [claim-016].

Note one explicit withdrawal of a number. Dimon retracted the \$20 billion capital deployment figure he had previously mentioned, saying the firm could do far more than that or nothing at all [claim-125].

## 4. Business drivers

**Markets, and Equities in particular, drove the quarter.** Equities revenue rose 86% year-on-year [claim-018] against Fixed Income's 6% [claim-017], with strength broad across products and regions [claim-019]. Management attributed it to a specific concurrence of events — major IPOs, index rebalancing, Korean equity market dynamics, heavy Asian activity and extremely active clients [claim-107] — and pointed out the drivers are externally observable in daily exchange and hedge fund volumes [claim-109].

**The banking franchise grew underneath the trading result.** The Commercial & Investment Bank reported net income of \$9.7 billion on revenue up 27% [claim-013]; Consumer & Community Banking \$5.3 billion on revenue up 8% [claim-008]; Asset & Wealth Management \$2 billion at a 38% pre-tax margin on revenue up 19% [claim-020], with long-term net inflows of \$50 billion [claim-021] and assets under management of \$5.1 trillion, up 18% [claim-022]. Net new checking accounts exceeded 500,000 in the quarter [claim-010].

**Deposits outperformed the firm's own expectations** [claim-037], attributed partly to franchise wins and partly to lending that creates deposits, notably non-bank financial institutions and data centre lending [claim-038].

**Capital intensity rose with the activity.** Risk-weighted assets increased approximately \$103 billion, largely from Markets financing and traditional lending growth [claim-006], taking the standardised CET1 ratio down 20 basis points to 14.1% [claim-005]. Management framed capital allocation as a response to client demand rather than an internal pot, with passive effects from higher volatility and rising equity markets [claim-159].

**AI is a cost and competitive question, not yet a margin story.** Dimon put AI capital expenditure across the economy at \$700 billion this year against \$400 billion last [claim-112], with forecasts a little over \$1 trillion next year [claim-113]. Internally there are almost 1,000 use cases of which he considers roughly 50 genuinely important [claim-102], and headcount has already fallen 30% to 40% in discrete areas [claim-103]. But management was explicit that the benefit accrues to customers rather than to the firm, because competitors get the same tools [claim-067][claim-104][claim-105]. Token expense was trivial in the first half and remains trivial for the full year, though it is forecast to accelerate [claim-154].

## 5. Base case

**Conditions:** capital markets activity normalises from an exceptional second quarter but stays above historical averages, the yield curve broadly follows the forwards, and consumer credit continues to perform as it did this quarter.

**Mechanism:** the Equities concurrence does not repeat [claim-034][claim-108], so Markets revenue steps down from an 86% growth rate [claim-018]; meanwhile the raised net interest income guidance [claim-047][claim-024] and the deposit balance strength behind it [claim-049] carry into the second half, and the improved card charge-off outlook [claim-026] holds.

**Consequence:** earnings step down sequentially from the second quarter while full-year guidance is met, with revenue-linked expense falling away alongside the revenue it is tied to [claim-090] — which is the same mechanism that produced the \$1.5 billion already booked [claim-087].

**Observable indicators:** exchange and hedge fund volumes, which management named as the daily read on its own drivers [claim-109]; whether investment banking fee growth holds, given management described the pipeline as still robust while acknowledging pull-forward into the quarter [claim-032][claim-015]; card net charge-offs tracking toward approximately 3.2% [claim-026].

**The strongest evidence against this base case** is not a bear argument but a definitional one. Revenue excluding significant items and expenses both grew 15% [claim-002][claim-003], so on a percentage basis the quarter produced no positive operating leverage [claim-196] — the precise point three analysts pressed [claim-063][claim-081][claim-095] and which management answered in dollar rather than percentage terms [claim-082][claim-084]. If the revenue step-down arrives while the fixed expense base and the investment programme continue [claim-043][claim-090], the percentage-basis picture worsens before the variable expense relief is visible. We still lead with the base case above because the expense increase is predominantly variable and already-earned [claim-088][claim-199], but a reader who weights the fixed component more heavily reaches a materially worse second half from the same evidence.

## 6. Upside case

**Conditions:** the capital markets environment persists rather than normalises, and the firm deploys excess capital into it.

**Mechanism:** management described the market as extremely risk-on with the firm largely a taker of that [claim-035], and Goldman's chief executive said momentum had accelerated with pipelines supporting continuation [claim-171]. Investment banking fees, by management's own account, are fine but not at peak by historical standards, leaving room to rise [claim-031]. The firm holds approximately \$40 billion of excess capital it now believes it can deploy [claim-122], targeted at a 17% return [claim-126], with large organic opportunities named in every business and specifically in Europe [claim-127].

**Consequence:** the fee line closes the gap on peers rather than lagging them — the one place the firm trailed this quarter [claim-195] — while deployed capital compounds at a return above the cost of holding it.

**Observable indicators:** investment banking fee growth converging on the 55% and 50% peers reported [claim-173][claim-179]; visible deployment of the excess capital rather than a further buyback-versus-hold debate [claim-120][claim-121]; Chase UK and the European build progressing, where management put UK customers at almost 3 million or 2.5 million [claim-136] and has done better than expected in Germany without yet reaching profitability [claim-137].

## 7. Downside case

**Conditions:** rates stay higher for longer, deposit repricing convexity finally appears, and the credit cycle turns while trading normalises.

**Mechanism:** management said the negative convexity of consumer deposit rate-paid has not yet appeared, contrary to what it would have predicted, but that common sense says it kicks in at some point [claim-077]. Barnum's own stress framing is explicit: ignoring convexity, a 7% rate environment could look attractive on reported earnings-at-risk and would not be if the deposit franchise had to be massively repriced to protect it [claim-078]. He also noted the firm's actual rate sensitivity is probably greater than its reported earnings-at-risk measure, because consumer deposit betas have outperformed the model [claim-050]. Simultaneously, Dimon confirmed a mild deterioration in competitor underwriting standards spread across revenue growth assumptions, expense add-backs and more payment-in-kind [claim-144], and said there will be a credit cycle in which performance is not a bell curve [claim-145].

**Consequence:** net interest income guidance premised on current deposit behaviour [claim-048] proves optimistic at the same time as the Markets contribution normalises and credit costs rise from the \$2.5 billion booked this quarter [claim-004].

**Observable warning signs:** deposit rate-paid accelerating, which the firm does assume in its stress work [claim-079]; the consumer cohort Barnum flagged with negative real wage growth in a higher-inflation environment [claim-118]; labour market deterioration, which he identified as what consumer credit ultimately comes down to [claim-119]; and data centre lending, which the firm treats as a bellwether and where it has already declined deals [claim-141], with relationship lending appearing to start-up borrowers building the centres [claim-143].

## 8. What to monitor

- **Equities repeatability.** Management twice said the combination of effects is statistically improbable to recur [claim-034][claim-108]. Exchange, CME and hedge fund volumes are the public read [claim-109].
- **Investment banking fee growth against peers.** The firm's 30% [claim-014] against Goldman's 55% [claim-173] and Bank of America's 50% [claim-179] is the clearest relative weakness in the quarter [claim-195].
- **The expense trajectory net of revenue-linked costs.** Whether the \$1 billion implied for the second half [claim-088] behaves as variable cost when revenue normalises.
- **Deposit rate-paid and balance behaviour**, given yield-seeking flows were already named as a modest risk to average balances per account [claim-040].
- **Card net charge-offs** against the approximately 3.2% guide [claim-026], and delinquency trends by FICO band [claim-116].
- **Capital deployment.** Whether the approximately \$40 billion [claim-122] is actually deployed at the stated 17% objective [claim-126], following the withdrawal of the \$20 billion figure [claim-125].
- **CEO succession.** Dimon characterised his remaining tenure as several years or a few years, plus or minus, with the decision resting with the Board [claim-062], after the Co-President appointments [claim-028] and Marianne Lake's retirement [claim-029].
- **Regulatory change** on the GSIB surcharge and short-term wholesale funding treatment [claim-071][claim-073], noting Barnum said he is not convinced regulation is currently a major drag on the firm's valuation [claim-148].

## 9. Uncertainties and missing evidence

**No company-specific guidance was given for Markets revenue**, which is the line that drove the quarter. The base case's step-down is therefore our inference from management's repeatability caution [claim-034][claim-108], not a disclosed expectation.

**Operating leverage has no agreed definition in this exchange.** Management asserted it was definitely positive [claim-082][claim-083] on a dollar basis [claim-084], while an analyst calculated it as negative in percentage terms on core revenue growth of 15% [claim-081] and another observed a negative year-to-date adjusted figure [claim-063]. Our claim-196 states the percentage-basis arithmetic; it does not adjudicate which definition is the right one, and readers should treat the disagreement as unresolved rather than settled.

**The marginal margin figure is an analyst's calculation, not the firm's.** The 77% marginal margin came from an analyst working off first-half figures management had just given [claim-095]; management did not confirm it.

**Pre-event consensus is not a single number.** This run captured two, at \$5.67 [claim-163] and \$5.52 [claim-167], with the forecast range spanning \$5.09 to \$6.56 [claim-164]. The beat is robust to either, but its magnitude is not a precise quantity, and consensus had itself risen 5.2% over the prior 30 days [claim-165][claim-193].

**Peer comparisons mix reporting bases.** Peer figures here come from each bank's own release or press coverage [claim-170][claim-178][claim-183][claim-188], not from a harmonised dataset, so the equities and fee growth comparisons [claim-194][claim-195] are directionally sound but not like-for-like on segment definitions.

**Two management positions were stated without supporting disclosure.** Barnum declined to discuss internal limit management [claim-158], and declined to disclose the marketing strategy affecting the expense line [claim-153].

**AI's effect on the cost base is unquantified.** Dimon said the firm cannot project the effects and that any slowdown in expense growth might only be visible in 2027 or 2028 [claim-065].
