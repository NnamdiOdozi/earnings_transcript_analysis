#### Key Takeaways

- Positive Sentiment: Strong Q1 results — statutory profit after tax was £1.6bn (RoTE 17%), net income £4.8bn (+9% YoY) and NII £3.6bn (NIM 3.17%, +7bps); management reiterated 2026 guidance and raised NII expectations to >£14.9bn with structural hedge income now guided >£7bn in 2026 and >£8bn in 2027.
- Positive Sentiment: Loan and balance-sheet momentum — lending rose to £486bn (+£5.1bn QoQ) with growth across mortgages and commercial, supporting income, while deposits were broadly stable (-£0.6bn) as the bank strategically limited participation in the fixed-term market to protect margins.
- Positive Sentiment: Cost discipline — operating costs fell 3% YoY to £2.5bn and the Q1 cost/income ratio was 51.9%; management remains confident of achieving a sub‑50% cost/income ratio for 2026 as income builds.
- Negative Sentiment: Provisioning and motor finance uncertainty — credit remains benign (Q1 impairment £295m; AQR 25bps, pre‑MES 16bps) but the group took a £101m net MES charge and left the GBP 1.95bn motor finance provision unchanged, warning the scenario‑based reserve could change if litigation or FCA outcomes differ.
- Positive Sentiment: Capital generation & returns — CET1 ended Q1 at 13.4% after 41bps generation; management expects >200bps capital generation for 2026, plans to pay down to ~13% by year‑end, and is executing a £1.75bn buyback (≈£700m completed).

## About Lloyds Banking Group

Lloyds Banking Group (NYSE:LYG) is a United Kingdom-based financial services company headquartered in London. Its principal businesses include retail banking, commercial banking, insurance, pensions and long-term savings. The group serves individuals, small and medium-sized businesses, commercial clients and institutional customers primarily across the United Kingdom.

The group operates through well-known brands including Lloyds Bank, Halifax, Bank of Scotland and Scottish Widows. Its products and services include current and savings accounts, mortgages, credit cards, personal loans, business banking, corporate lending, investment products, insurance and retirement planning. Lloyds Banking Group also provides digital banking services through its online and mobile platforms.

Lloyds Banking Group was created in 2009 through the integration of Lloyds TSB and HBOS during the global financial crisis. Lloyds Bank traces its origins to 1765, while Halifax and Bank of Scotland have histories extending back several centuries. The group is led by Chief Executive Officer Charlie Nunn and provides services through branches, digital channels and other distribution networks throughout the UK.
