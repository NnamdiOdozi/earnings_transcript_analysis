# Outlook Brief: LLOY — 2026-h1 (30 June 2026)

## 1. Outlook in brief

For the 6 months to 30 Jun 2026, Lloyds Banking Group reported statutory profit
after tax of £3.1 billion and a return on tangible equity of 17.1%, on net income
of £9.7 billion, up 9% year-on-year [claim-001][claim-002]. Alongside the results,
management unveiled a new five-year plan, Accelerate 2030, and stepped up capital
returns materially: a 30% increase in the interim dividend plus a new £1 billion
interim buyback, together over £1.9 billion of H1 distributions
[claim-009][claim-010][claim-027]. The near-term direction is constructive:
underlying momentum in net interest income, other income and credit quality all
support the unchanged 2026 guidance, and the announced growth in dividend and
buybacks signals management's confidence in that trajectory continuing into H2
2026 and beyond [claim-016][claim-021][claim-006]. The more material judgment
call is not 2026 — where guidance was simply reaffirmed — but the shape of the
2027-2030 Accelerate 2030 plan, where net income and cost targets rest on several
explicitly "non-heroic" or conservative assumptions that management has chosen not
to fully unwind [claim-047][claim-048].

## 2. What changed

This was as much a strategy-update event as a results release, so "what changed"
splits into in-period performance and newly stated multi-year targets. On
performance, H1 2026 net income growth of 9% [claim-002] and net interest income
growth of 9% [claim-016] both ran ahead of the flat cost base [claim-004],
producing positive operating leverage — cost:income ratio of 50.4% for H1 and 49%
in Q2, already inside the below 50 percent full-year guidance band [claim-005][claim-022].
Cumulative delivery against the outgoing 2022-2026 plan was also disclosed for the
first time in final form: over £2 billion of gross cost savings and £28 billion of
RWA optimisation delivered, both ahead of original targets, and circa £2 billion of
strategic-initiative revenue reached early [claim-028][claim-029][claim-030].

The genuinely new information is the Accelerate 2030 guidance set: a mid-single-
digit net income CAGR and high-single-digit OOI CAGR to 2030, a cost:income ratio
falling to below 45% by 2030, RoTE of circa 20% by 2030 (more than 18% by 2028),
and capital generation building from more than 200bps in 2026 to more than 225bps
by 2030 [claim-033][claim-034][claim-039][claim-040][claim-041][claim-042]. The
2028 RoTE guidance of "more than 18%" was explicitly flagged by an analyst as
light versus consensus expectations, and management's response leaned heavily on
the assumptions being conservative rather than disputing the comparison
[claim-047][claim-048] — that exchange is one of the more informative data points
in the whole call (see Section 9).

## 3. Management guidance

2026 (reaffirmed, not changed this call): net interest income greater than £14.9
billion [claim-020]; structural hedge income greater than £7 billion
[claim-018]; asset quality ratio around 25bps [claim-023]; return on tangible
equity more than 16% [claim-024]; CET1 paid down to 13% by year end
[claim-025]; capital generation more than 200bps [claim-026].

2027-2030 (new, Accelerate 2030): hedge income greater than £8 billion in 2027
[claim-019]; total cash investment up around 10-15% in 2027 versus 2026, with
cumulative cash investment in excess of £13 billion over the plan
[claim-035][claim-036]; a further circa £2 billion of gross cost savings from 2027
to 2030 [claim-037]; asset quality ratio guided at 25-30bps throughout the plan
[claim-038]; cost:income ratio below 45% by 2030 [claim-033]; RoTE more than 18%
in 2028 rising to circa 20% by 2030 [claim-041]; capital generation rising from
more than 200bps (2026) to around 225bps (2028) to beyond 225bps (2030)
[claim-042]. Management explicitly declined to quantify AI's revenue and cost contribution
to the 2027-2030 targets beyond a narrowly defined in-year 2026 benefit figure
disclosed only qualitatively on this call, deferring a fuller AI framework to the
January 2027 year-end results (see Section 9) — this is a genuine gap in an
otherwise fully quantified guidance set, not an oversight on our part.

## 4. Business drivers

**Balance sheet growth** is broad-based rather than concentrated: group lending up
£5 billion (1%) and deposits up £5 billion (1%) quarter-on-quarter, with
commercial balances up £3.1 billion and open-book assets under administration
(AUA) up £25 billion to £251 billion, all in Q2 alone [claim-011][claim-013]
[claim-014][claim-015]. **Margin** is a modest tailwind: net interest margin rose
to 322bps in Q2 from 319bps for H1, and mortgage completion margins ticked up to
just over 70bps [claim-017][claim-012] — management was explicit that it has not
assumed this margin improvement persists or grows over the plan [claim-047].
**Cost discipline** is the other pillar of the current-year story: flat H1 opex
[claim-004] sits against management's own admission that opex growth will step up
to roughly 3% in 2027 as investment is "reignited" [claim-048], meaning 2026's
flat cost base is a trough, not the new run rate. **Regulatory/macro**: the plan
is built on a below-market terminal rate assumption of 3.5% and a hedge
refinancing rate of 3.7% — both explicitly below current market-implied levels
— plus an anticipated Basel 3.1 RWA reduction at the start of 2027
[claim-044][claim-045][claim-043]. **Competitive dynamics**: management does not
see the market as structurally more competitive than in recent years, but
acknowledges ongoing margin pressure on balance-sheet-heavy products (deposits,
mortgages) that is bundled into the plan's stated "income headwinds"
[claim-051][claim-046].

## 5. Base case

The most plausible path is that Lloyds delivers on its reaffirmed 2026 guidance
(NII, hedge income, AQR, RoTE, capital generation) largely unchanged from what was
already flagged before this call, since H1 trends were already tracking to plan on
every metric disclosed [claim-016][claim-006][claim-007]. Over the 2027-2030
Accelerate 2030 horizon, the more likely outcome is delivery toward the upper half
of the stated ranges rather than a miss, because management has repeatedly
characterised its own assumptions as conservative on both the NII/hedge side
(3.7% refinancing rate versus a market already ~50bps higher) and the RoTE/capital
side (no credit taken for the hedge/market rate gap in the circa 20% 2030 RoTE
target) [claim-047][claim-034][claim-042]. That combination — genuinely conservative inputs plus a
demonstrated track record of beating or meeting the prior five-year plan's targets
[claim-029][claim-030][claim-028] — is what underpins a constructive base case
rather than a merely neutral one.

## 6. Upside case

If market interest rates settle above the plan's 3.5% terminal-rate/3.7%
refinancing-rate assumptions — as they currently are, by management's own
admission [claim-047][claim-045] — structural hedge income could land above the
greater than £8 billion 2027 framing management gave [claim-019] without
committing to a precise figure, since the hedge notional and yield gap directly
determine that income. The observable indicator would be the quarterly
hedge yield print (last given as trending toward the 3.7% refinancing rate
[claim-047]) failing to compress even as market rates hold, alongside continued
NIM expansion beyond the 322bps Q2 print [claim-017]. A second upside path is
faster-than-guided delivery on the further £2 billion of 2027-2030 gross cost
saves [claim-037], which management said it would "out-deliver" and return to
shareholders if capacity allowed, rather than reinvest into higher costs
[claim-034] — a rising buyback cadence beyond the current £1 billion interim
[claim-010] would be the confirming signal.

## 7. Downside case

The clearest downside path runs through the "income headwinds" management has
already bundled into guidance rather than broken out in full: continued SVR
mortgage-book runoff and competitive margin pressure on deposits and mortgages
[claim-032][claim-046]. If competitive intensity increases beyond what's assumed
— for instance if peers with currently strong returns (Barclays and Standard
Chartered both reported profit growth ahead of expectations this same period
[claim-053][claim-054][claim-055]) chase volume more aggressively — margin
compression could outpace the structural hedge tailwind earlier than the "broad
plateau, maybe coming off a touch" NIM path management described for the back half
of the plan. The observable warning sign would be mortgage completion margins
(currently just over 70bps [claim-012]) or deposit margins compressing quarter on
quarter rather than holding flat. A second downside path is execution risk on the
2027 investment step-up itself: cash investment is guided up 10-15% in 2027
[claim-036], and if the associated cost savings mature more slowly than the
"progressively more, second half of the plan" pattern management described, the
45% 2030 cost:income target [claim-033] would come under pressure without a
corresponding revenue offset.

## 8. What to monitor

- Quarterly structural hedge yield versus the 3.7% refinancing-rate assumption,
  and hedge notional growth versus the "pretty modest" guidance
  [claim-047].
- Mortgage completion margins and deposit margins, as the most sensitive
  indicators of the competitive-pressure assumption embedded in guidance
  [claim-012][claim-046].
- 2027 opex growth print versus the "around 3%" guided step-up, and whether
  the associated cost savings mature on the stated back-half-of-plan schedule
  [claim-048][claim-037].
- Any quantification of AI revenue/cost contribution at the January 2027
  year-end results, which management explicitly deferred this call.
- Progress of the £2 billion 2027-2030 gross cost-save programme against the
  "out-deliver, then return to shareholders" framing [claim-034][claim-037].
- Buyback cadence beyond the current £1 billion interim as a signal of
  capital-generation upside being realised [claim-010][claim-042].

## 9. Uncertainties and missing evidence

Management declined to give a full 2030 AI revenue/cost quantification, citing
uncertainty over customer adoption and regulatory approval as the two variables it
cannot control, and promised more detail at the January 2027 year-end results —
this is a disclosed, acknowledged gap rather than an omission we are inferring.
The RoTE-versus-consensus exchange (2028 guidance of "more than 18%" [claim-041]
characterised by an analyst as light versus consensus) is informative but
incomplete: management
walked through several conservative assumptions (hedge refinancing rate, deposit
margins, RWA density) without stating what the "as-modelled" RoTE would be absent
that conservatism [claim-047] — so the size of the potential upside cannot be
quantified from this run's evidence, only its direction. Two of the three peer
data points used above (Barclays, Standard Chartered) are reliable pre-event
comparators, dated 28-29 July 2026, ahead of Lloyds' 30 July call
[claim-053][claim-054]; a broader analyst-consensus estimate for Lloyds itself
was available in the run's web evidence but carried no reliable publication date
relative to the call, so it has deliberately not been used as a pre-event
consensus figure in this brief or in claims.json — that is a limitation of the
web-search evidence, not a gap in the transcript.
