## Summary

Microsoft reported a record close to fiscal year 2026 with Q4 revenue of $90.0 billion, up 18% YoY (17% in constant currency), exceeding expectations. Operating income grew 18% to $40.5 billion, with operating margins expanding slightly to 45%. Earnings per share of $4.74 (up 23% adjusted for OpenAI-related items) beat guidance by $0.27, driven by a $3.2 billion gain on Anthropic investment and lower-than-expected voluntary retirement expenses, partially offset by Xbox severance and impairments. The Microsoft Cloud surpassed $214 billion in annual revenue, growing 27%, with Azure alone exceeding $100 billion for the fiscal year, up 41%. Capital expenditures reached $41 billion in the quarter, with 31 new data centers added, reflecting accelerating AI demand. Management highlighted strong execution in Azure, which grew 43% (ahead of guidance), driven by efficiency gains and rapid monetization of incremental capacity, but noted demand continues to outpace supply. Microsoft 365 Copilot paid seats more than doubled sequentially to over 30 million, with the new E7 suite gaining traction and usage-based billing expanding. Looking ahead to Q1 FY2027, management guided revenue of $89.85-$ 90.95 billion (16-17% growth), with Azure expected to accelerate to ~45% constant-currency growth. CapEx is expected to exceed $50 billion in Q1, elevated due to lease reclassification from the extension of data center useful lives to 25 years. Risks include ongoing capacity constraints, higher component pricing, and PC market weakness (Windows OEM expected to decline low-20s in Q1). Opportunities include continued AI platform adoption, expanding TAM via consumption-based models, and the new Frontier Company initiative to embed engineers with 164 enterprise customers.

## Operations

### Financial Performance

- Revenue: $90.0B (+18% YoY, +17% CC). Microsoft Cloud: $59.3B (+27% YoY).
- Gross Margin: 67% (down YoY due to mix shift to Azure and AI infrastructure investments, partially offset by efficiency gains).
- Operating Income: $40.5B (+18% YoY). Operating margin: 45% (up slightly YoY).
- Earnings Per Share: $4.74 (+23% adjusted for OpenAI items).
- Cash Flow: Operating cash flow $55.4B (+30% YoY); free cash flow $19.6B (impacted by elevated CapEx).
- Capital Expenditures: $41B in Q4 (two-thirds short-lived assets: CPUs/GPUs). Cash paid for PP&E: $35.8B. Finance leases: $5.6B.

### Segment & Product Analysis

#### Productivity and Business Processes ($37.8B, +14%)

- M365 Commercial Cloud: +16% adjusted (normalized for prior-year in-period recognition). Paid seats grew 6% YoY; Copilot paid seats exceeded 30M (net adds more than doubled QoQ). Premium SKUs (E5, E7) drove ARPU growth.
- M365 Consumer Cloud: +24% (22% CC). Subscriptions grew 7%.
- LinkedIn: +12% (10% CC), driven by marketing solutions.
- Dynamics 365: +13% (12% CC). ERP bookings healthy; CRM sales cycles lengthening.
- Segment Gross Margin: +14% (13% CC); margin down slightly due to Copilot usage costs.
- Operating Income: +15% (14% CC); margin 58%.

#### Intelligent Cloud ($39.3B, +32%)

- Azure and Other Cloud Services: +43% ahead of guidance. Demand exceeds supply; efficiency gains and process improvements enabled rapid monetization of incremental capacity. GitHub Copilot benefited from June pricing change to usage-based model.
- On-Premises Server: Revenue flat YoY (down 1% CC), slightly above expectations due to contract mix.
- Segment Gross Margin: +24%; margin down YoY due to Azure mix and AI infrastructure scaling. GitHub margins improved through the quarter after pricing change.
- Operating Income: +31%; margin 41% (flat YoY).

#### More Personal Computing ($12.9B, -4%)

- Windows OEM & Devices: -7% (Windows OEM -5%), impacted by lower PC demand and high prior-year comparable from Windows 10 end-of-support; slightly ahead of expectations due to channel inventory build.
- Search & News Advertising ex TAC: +10% (9% CC), driven by higher revenue per search and volume.
- Xbox Content & Services: -10% against strong prior-year first-party content. Hardware revenue declined.
- Segment Gross Margin: -2%; margin up YoY due to lower Activision amortization.
- Operating Income: -14%; margin 21%.

### Geographic & Customer Metrics

- Commercial RPO grew 84% to $678B (including OpenAI); excluding OpenAI, RPO +25% with weighted average duration of 2.3 years. ~30% recognized in next 12 months (+37% YoY).
- Cloud revenue: nearly 90% from non-Frontier Model companies.
- Bookings grew 18% excluding OpenAI impact, driven by core annuity sales across all geographies and customer segments.

## Outlook

### Q1 FY2027 Guidance (quarter ending September 2026)

- Revenue: $89.85B - $90.95B (+16-17% YoY).
- COGS: $29.6B - $29.8B (+23-24% YoY).
- Operating Expense: $16.8B - $16.9B (+7-8% YoY).
- Operating Margin: Relatively flat YoY.
- Effective Tax Rate: ~20%.
- Other Income & Expense: ~ -$100 million (excluding OpenAI).
- Capital Expenditures: ~$50B+ (includes impact from lease reclassification due to useful life extension to 25 years for data centers and office buildings).
- FX Impact: Reduces total revenue growth by <1 point; no meaningful impact on COGS/opex.

#### Segment Guidance

- Productivity & Business Processes: $36.7B - $37.0B (growth 11-12%). M365 Commercial Cloud growth ~16% CC adjusted (15% reported). M365 Commercial Products mid-single-digit growth. M365 Consumer Cloud mid-teens growth. LinkedIn high-single-digit growth. Dynamics 365 low-teens growth.
- Intelligent Cloud: $40.95B - $41.25B (growth 33-34%). Azure growth ~45% CC (H1 expected to accelerate); on-premises server low- to mid-single-digit decline.
- More Personal Computing: $12.2B - $12.7B. Windows OEM & Devices decline low-20s. Search ex-TAC mid-single-digit growth. Xbox content & services mid-single-digit decline.

#### Full-Year FY2027 Commentary

- Revenue and operating income expected to grow double digits.
- Operating expenses mid-to-high single-digit growth.
- CapEx to grow YoY.
- Full-year operating margins down less than 1 point.
- Free cash flow positive.
- Effective tax rate ~20%.
- FX: reduces full-year revenue growth by <1 point.
- M365 Commercial Products and Server Products: revenue declines mid-single digits due to lapping transactional purchasing.
- Windows OEM & Devices: revenue declines high-teens due to lower PC demand, higher component costs, and elevated inventory.

#### Key Assumptions

- Customer demand continues to exceed supply, particularly in Azure.
- Efficiency gains and process improvements will help bridge capacity gaps.
- New usage-based billing models (Copilot, GitHub Copilot) will contribute incremental revenue.
- Extension of data center useful life to 25 years shifts some finance leases to operating leases, reducing reported CapEx but not changing underlying investment plans; minimal benefit to FY27 operating income.

## Q&A Highlights

Q (Karl Keirstead, UBS): Satya, elaborate on model choice and protection of corporate IP. How material could open/custom models be over 1-2 years? How does Microsoft benefit given its Frontier Lab exposure?

A (Satya Nadella): Enterprises need to control their own destiny by building their learning machine. Models are an input, not an extraction of enterprise knowledge. Our platform architecture separates the harness (memory, context, actions) from the model, making any model swappable. Customers can use frontier models for high-value tasks and lower-cost models or train their own for specific needs. We ourselves use this pattern in Copilot, GitHub Copilot, and Security Copilot. The Azure infrastructure is fungible and efficient regardless of the model chosen. This approach ensures resilience, cost control, and choice.

A (Amy Hood): The platform is designed to deliver the right model for the right job on Azure. Growing demand for AI workloads benefits Azure regardless of the model family chosen.

---

Q (Brent Thill, Jefferies): Amy, Azure accelerated to 43%, heading to mid-40s. What are the underlying drivers? Are capacity constraints still an issue, or is Microsoft just executing better?

A (Amy Hood): Demand continues to exceed available supply. The improvement came from efficiency gains in both CPU and GPU fleets, as well as process improvements that shortened lead times to bring capacity online. When we make efficiency gains, they are quickly monetized due to the supply-demand imbalance. This dynamic drove the acceleration in Q4 and is part of the expected Q1 trajectory.

---

Q (Mark Moerdler, Bernstein Research): Concern about oversupply and component pricing increasing. How does Microsoft manage through hardware price increases without driving up prices or harming margins?

A (Amy Hood): Most CapEx is in short-lived assets (CPUs/GPUs) with short lead times; if demand changes, we can slow down that spend. Investment in land and buildings is more flexible—timing can be adjusted. We have a diverse book of business by geo, segment, and industry, providing flexibility. The cloud still offers great ROI vs. on-premises. We focus on efficiency to maintain value for customers while adjusting pricing in new contracts where needed.

A (Satya Nadella): It's important to get the product shape, portfolio mix, and customer mix right, and run an efficient railroad. We work monotonically on efficiency. The secular shift is clear, and we are bullish on our margin structure and customer value.

---

Q (Adam Wood, Morgan Stanley): M365 Copilot strong with 30M paid seats. How are customers moving from pilots to broader deployments? What drives monetization: additional seats, higher SKUs like E7, or consumption?

A (Satya Nadella): The product shape has changed—now includes Chat, CoWork, Autopilot, Code. Time to high usage has fallen from months to days. Usage intensity is now comparable to Outlook/Teams. The enterprise wiring with Agent 365 (IT, SecOps, FinOps) and business process integration drives compound usage. The business model is seat plus usage. ARPU growth from E7 is already visible. The TAM is more expansive than traditional Office due to enterprise-wide applicability and usage-based pricing.

A (Amy Hood): ARPU growth from E5+ Copilot and now E7 (with Agent 365 component) is encouraging. E7 provides observability and manageability of token spend. Usage-based billing will expand TAM further.

---

Q (Brad Zelnick, Deutsche Bank): Cybersecurity playing field shifted with new frontier model releases. What does Project Perception mean for the cyber business and trust in Microsoft?

A (Satya Nadella): The physics of cyber operations have changed. Perception is an agentic system with red team, blue team, and green team agents that continuously find, triage, and fix vulnerabilities. It leverages signals from Entra, Defender, network, etc., for context. Multi-model approach is critical—e.g., using MAI-Cyber-1-Flash for 90% of tasks and a frontier model for 10% reduces cost by 50% while maintaining performance. This also provides resilience if a model becomes unavailable. We are excited about Perception's potential for our security business.

---

Q (Gabriela Borges, Goldman Sachs): Amy, put CapEx and monetization together. How does ROI on CapEx decisions compare to a year ago? What levers can you pull (e.g., internal silicon) for incremental monetization?

A (Amy Hood): My math hasn't changed, but confidence in TAM expansion and margin levers has increased. Levers include product improvements, infrastructure efficiency, silicon price-performance (including first-party), model diversification for cost efficiency, and the broad portfolio across knowledge work, coding, security, and the agent layer. All contribute to improved ROIC. It's a daily grind to get better, and we are good at that.

Q follow-up: That makes sense. Thank you.
