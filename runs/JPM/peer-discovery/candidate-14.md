### JPMorgan Chase & Co. (JPM)

JPMorgan is the largest US bank by assets, operating in consumer banking, commercial banking, asset and wealth management, and corporate and investment banking. The company is widely considered the best-run money-center bank, with consistent return on tangible common equity well above peers.

Dimon-era discipline on credit, capital and technology spending has produced sustained share gains across most segments.

### Bank of America Corporation (BAC)

Bank of America is the second-largest US bank, with consumer banking, global wealth and investment management (including Merrill), global banking and global markets segments. The bank has a particularly large US consumer deposit franchise.

A legacy held-to-maturity securities book purchased at low yields continues to drag net interest income relative to peers.

### JPM: structural strengths

Best-in-class return on tangible common equity, scale advantages in deposits and trading, and a fortress balance sheet that earns a premium multiple to peers.

### BAC: structural strengths

A very large US consumer deposit franchise, leading position in wealth management via Merrill and meaningful operating leverage as the bond book rolls over.

### Risks for JPM

- - Net interest income sensitive to Fed policy.
- - Credit-cycle exposure across consumer and commercial.
- - Regulatory capital requirements remain a constraint.
- - Investment-banking revenue is cyclical.

### Risks for BAC

- - Held-to-maturity securities portfolio is a persistent drag.
- - Credit-cycle exposure.
- - Regulatory capital constraints.
- - Net interest income sensitivity to deposit migration.

## Which is right for you?

The honest answer is "it depends on what kind of investor you are". Both JPM and BAC have credible long-term theses, but they reward different temperaments and different portfolio roles.

If you are a growth investor prioritizing earnings acceleration, the question is which company has the larger ceiling and the cleaner near-term catalysts. Quality-focused investors who want the highest-quality money-center bank. Value investors who view the duration drag as a temporary problem.

If you are a value investor focused on the multiple paid versus the cash returned, look at the P/E and free-cash-flow context in the table above plus the capital-return policy of each name. Where one has a more aggressive buyback or a higher dividend yield, that matters more than headline growth.

If you are a dividend investor concerned with stable income, the qualitative payout discipline of each business is the key signal. Some names on this page pay meaningful dividends with long growth records; others do not pay any dividend at all. Read the risk section carefully to gauge dividend safety, not just yield.
