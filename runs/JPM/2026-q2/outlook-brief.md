# Outlook Brief: JPM — 2026-q2 (30 June 2026)

## 1. Outlook in brief

This run's headline figures cover the **3 months to 30 Jun 2026** (fiscal Q2 2026). JPMorgan
delivered a strong quarter that beat the Street: adjusted EPS of \$6.14 [claim-001] against a
\$5.67 consensus [claim-022], a clear beat [claim-027], on revenue up 15% ex-significant-items
[claim-002] and an ROTCE of 23% [claim-001]. The standout driver was the Commercial &
Investment Bank — net income \$9.7B on revenue up 27% [claim-008], with equities revenue up 86%
[claim-011] and IB fees up 30% [claim-009]. Into the next period, management guides full-year
2026 total NII to about \$105.5B [claim-016] and adjusted expense to about \$107.5B [claim-017].
The near-term question is how much of the exceptional Markets/IB strength is sustainable.

## 2. What changed

- **Beat vs expectations:** adjusted EPS \$6.14 [claim-001] came in well above the \$5.67
  consensus [claim-022], a clear beat [claim-027].
- **Capital return raised:** the Board intends to increase the quarterly dividend to \$1.65 per
  share from the third quarter [claim-006].
- **Card credit outlook improved:** the full-year Card net charge-off rate is now expected at
  about 3.2%, reflecting better-than-expected consumer credit [claim-018].
- **Expense guidance raised:** management revised up this year's expense guidance by \$2.5B, a
  mildly-negative offset to the revenue strength [claim-029].

## 3. Management guidance

Management gave full-year 2026 guidance: total NII of approximately \$105.5B (NII ex-Markets
about \$96.5B, Markets NII about \$9B) [claim-016]; adjusted expense of about \$107.5B
[claim-017]; and a Card net charge-off rate of about 3.2% [claim-018]. Capital return guidance:
the quarterly dividend rises to \$1.65 per share in Q3 [claim-006].

## 4. Business drivers

- **Markets and Investment Banking.** CIB net income \$9.7B, revenue up 27% [claim-008]; equities
  up 86% [claim-011]; Fixed Income up 6% [claim-010]; IB fees up 30% [claim-009]. This is the
  engine of the quarter.
- **Consumer (CCB).** Net income \$5.3B, revenue up 8% [claim-007], with 500,000+ net new
  checking accounts and client investment assets up 21% [claim-015].
- **Asset & Wealth Management.** Net income \$2B, revenue up 19%, pre-tax margin 38% [claim-012];
  AUM \$5.1T up 18% [claim-013] with \$50B long-term net inflows [claim-014].
- **Capital & credit.** CET1 14.1% [claim-005]; credit costs \$2.5B with a small \$149M reserve
  build [claim-004]; Dimon called the deterioration in underwriting standards "very mild"
  [claim-021].

## 5. Base case

Continued double-digit revenue strength near-term, led by CIB [claim-008], with consumer
[claim-007] and AWM [claim-012] steady. NII lands around the \$105.5B full-year guide [claim-016]
and expenses near \$107.5B [claim-017]. Capital return steps up via the higher dividend
[claim-006] and disciplined buybacks [claim-020]. Credit stays benign, consistent with the
improved 3.2% Card charge-off outlook [claim-018] and Dimon's "very mild" characterisation
[claim-021]. This is comfortably ahead of the pre-print consensus posture [claim-022], given the
beat [claim-027].

## 6. Upside case

Markets and IB momentum persists rather than normalising: sustained equities strength beyond the
+86% print [claim-011] and IB fee growth above +30% [claim-009] would push CIB revenue above the
+27% run-rate [claim-008]. Better-than-guided credit (Card charge-offs below 3.2% [claim-018])
and NII above \$105.5B [claim-016] would compound it. Broad big-bank strength this quarter —
Citigroup net income up 45% [claim-028], Bank of America up 27% [claim-023], Goldman Sachs net
earnings \$6.63B [claim-026] — suggests the capital-markets tailwind is industry-wide, not
JPMorgan-specific, supporting persistence.

## 7. Downside case

The clearest risk is that the exceptional Markets/IB quarter is a peak, not a plateau — equities
+86% [claim-011] is unlikely to repeat, and Dimon himself said conditions are "close to as good
as it gets… we just don't know how long it's going to last" [claim-019]. A rate-driven NII
squeeze against the \$105.5B guide [claim-016], or credit normalising faster than the "very mild"
view [claim-021] with the Card charge-off rate drifting above 3.2% [claim-018], would pressure
earnings. Warning signs: CIB revenue decelerating from +27% [claim-008]; reserve builds larger
than the \$149M this quarter [claim-004].

## 8. What to monitor

- CIB revenue trajectory vs the +27% base [claim-008], and whether equities [claim-011] and IB
  fees [claim-009] hold up.
- NII against the \$105.5B full-year guide [claim-016] as rate expectations move.
- Card net charge-off rate vs the 3.2% outlook [claim-018] and the size of reserve builds
  [claim-004] as a credit-normalisation signal.
- CET1 [claim-005] and the pace of capital return (dividend [claim-006], buybacks [claim-020]).
- AWM net inflows [claim-014] and AUM [claim-013] as a fee-durability signal.

## 9. Uncertainties and missing evidence

- **Adjusted vs GAAP.** The \$6.14 EPS and \$16.9B net income management highlighted are
  *ex-significant-items* [claim-001]; JPMorgan's GAAP diluted EPS for the quarter was higher (the
  SEC 10-Q records \$7.70). The beat-vs-consensus inference [claim-027] compares an adjusted
  actual to a consensus whose basis is not fully specified in the cited source [claim-022].
- **Peer vintage.** The peer figures [claim-023][claim-024][claim-025][claim-026][claim-028] are
  the peers' own Q2 2026 results, all reported around JPMorgan's 14 Jul 2026 call — Citigroup and
  Wells Fargo the same morning, and Dimon noted on the call that he had already read Goldman
  Sachs's numbers that morning. So the peer results were broadly knowable as same-day competitive
  backdrop; they are still used here as backdrop, not as figures management's own results depended
  on.
- **Sustainability.** No explicit full-year revenue or ROTCE target was given beyond the NII and
  expense guides [claim-016][claim-017], so the durability of the Markets/IB surge is a judgement,
  not a disclosed number.
